Which Flux turn-taking setting fits this audio?

Every threshold trades interrupting people for waiting too long. This report measures that trade on real recordings with Deepgram Flux, then checks how the chosen setting holds up when someone else is talking in the room.

With someone else talking in the room

The same calls were re-run with another person's conversation mixed in behind the speaker. At the default setting, the share of turns Flux kept open for more than 1.5 seconds went from 7% to 61%, and 20% of turns never ended before the user spoke again. No threshold fixes this: raising it removes cut-offs but leaves nearly every turn open.

How this was measured

Real passes, not simulation

Flux reports its end-of-turn confidence every 240 ms but decides on 80 ms frames, so replaying the reported confidence against a new threshold misses some decisions and drifts badly on multi-turn audio. Every number above comes from a real Flux pass at that threshold.

Turn boundaries

A turn ends at the speaker's last word before the agent starts talking, using Flux's own word timestamps. That matches how people label turns.

Strict count

A turn counts as cut off when Flux fires more than 100 ms before the end of the speaker's last word. Some of those fire during the final word itself, with nothing said afterwards, which a listener may not notice. Counting only cut-offs where the speaker said at least one more word, the default's rate is 22.9% instead of 26.2%. The table view under the trade-off chart gives both counts for every setting.

What this report can and cannot say

The speech is spontaneous meeting talk from the AMI corpus (CC BY 4.0), with longer thinking pauses than most replies to a voice agent, so it is a hard case. Each speaker's turns were cut from a meeting and separated by silence standing in for the agent. The background talker is a second AMI speaker mixed in digitally, not a real room.
