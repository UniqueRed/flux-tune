How this was measured

Real passes, not simulation

Flux reports its end-of-turn confidence every 240 ms but decides on 80 ms frames, so replaying the reported confidence against a new threshold misses some decisions and drifts badly on multi-turn audio. Every number above comes from a real Flux pass at that threshold.

Turn boundaries

A turn ends at the speaker's last word before the agent starts talking, using Flux's own word timestamps. That matches how people label turns.

Strict count

A turn counts as cut off when Flux fires more than 100 ms before the end of the speaker's last word. Some of those fire during the final word itself, with nothing said afterwards, which a listener may not notice. Counting only cut-offs where the speaker said at least one more word, the default's rate is 6.3% instead of 15.1%. The table view under the trade-off chart gives both counts for every setting.

What this report can and cannot say

SpokenWOZ (CC BY-NC 4.0) is crowdworkers role-playing customer and agent on real phone calls, recorded at 8 kHz; Flux receives the customer channel upsampled to 16 kHz. Turn boundaries come from the dataset's annotated agent turns. The calls were split into two halves (19 to tune, 20 to report) and confidence intervals resample whole calls. Phone-band audio likely loosens word timings, which is why the strict count above differs so much from the headline cut-off rate. There is no background-talker test on this set.
